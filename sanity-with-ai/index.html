What I built

Security engineers burn out on toil: thousands of alerts that are mostly noise, a growing ticket backlog, raw logs at 3 a.m. and audit evidence gathered by screenshot. The result is missed threats, slow fixes and tired people. This project is a working loop that moves the volume to AI without handing it the decisions.

Alerts from SIEM, EDR, CSPM and tickets are de-duplicated, filtered by tuning rules that each have an owner and an expiry date, correlated across sources on the same user or host, and ranked by severity, asset criticality and confidence. Each incident is enriched with its owner, exposure and blast radius: the crown-jewel systems within reach. The AI drafts what happened, why it matters, and which actions from a fixed catalog fit. A named human approves, rejects or escalates. Approved actions run with a credential that holds only that action's permission.

The same loop builds incident timelines from alerts and chat, with secrets redacted. It also turns configuration snapshots into a hashed evidence pack mapped to SOC 2, ISO 27001 and NIST CSF. When a human rejects a false positive, it becomes an expiring suppression, so the next run is quieter.

Guardrails

  • Human approves every action: AI identities are refused at decide and act
  • Least privilege: read-only AI identity, one scoped credential per action
  • Prompt injection: alert text is untrusted. A planted payload is flagged, and a compromised model cannot close the incident
  • No secrets or PII in prompts, and only approved models
  • Hash-chained audit log of every suggestion, decision and action
